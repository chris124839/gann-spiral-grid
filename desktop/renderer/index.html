01

Square of Nine

A numerical spiral used by some Gann practitioners to derive candidate price and time relationships. Different alignments and scaling conventions can produce different levels.

Learning sequence

  1. Understand cell numbering and the spiral.
  2. Learn cardinal and diagonal crosses.
  3. Select a meaningful pivot and document the alignment.
  4. Test candidate levels against historical pivots.
Restriction: a mathematical level does not establish direction, timing or probability by itself.
02

Gann Angles

Geometric lines relate price movement to elapsed time. Their interpretation depends on chart scaling and the chosen time unit.

Checklist

  1. Record the origin pivot.
  2. Specify the price/time scale.
  3. Record the angle convention.
  4. Test breaks and retests without changing scale after the fact.
03

Price & Time

Compare predefined price levels and time windows with actual market pivots. Record both successful and unsuccessful forecasts.

Checklist

  1. Choose the cycle before reviewing the outcome.
  2. Define the pivot window in advance.
  3. Include costs and missed signals.
  4. Use out-of-sample testing.
04

Rule Breaker Lab

Separate a calculation error, a failed method and an execution error.

Wrong pivot or scaleRecalculate consistently; do not select a new anchor only because the result failed.
Price breaks the levelRecord an invalidation if the predefined rule says so. Do not average down automatically.
Cycle date passesRecord a miss; do not move the forecast date after seeing the market.
